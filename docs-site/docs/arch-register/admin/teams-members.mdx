# Teams And Members

Teams and members are related, but they are not the same administrative concern.

Manage both from **Workspace Settings → People**. Workspace administrators with the relevant people-management
permissions can add or remove members, assign workspace roles, create teams, and assign team roles. Platform
administrators can create local user accounts from Members only when local authentication is enabled; see
[Identity and Authentication](identity-and-authentication).

Member and team actions depend on the corresponding workspace capabilities, such as `people.invite`, `people.remove`,
`people.role`, and `people.teams`. A user's global account role does not automatically make them a member of each
workspace.

## Teams

Teams define ownership boundaries for entities and projects. A team membership does not automatically make someone a
workspace member; the person must also belong to the workspace.

Use teams to:

- assign responsibility
- control team-scoped access to owned entities and projects
- keep ownership aligned with the people doing the work

Add or edit teams from **Workspace Settings → Teams**. Assign workspace users to a team and give each person a team
role. Team administrators and editors can edit team-owned entities; reviewers can view them. Team administrators can
edit and delete team-owned projects, and team editors can edit them. Use team ownership to answer who is responsible,
and the workspace role to control broader workspace capabilities.

## Members

Members are the users who belong to the workspace. Member management is where you assign workspace roles.

<ThemedImage
  alt="Members screen in Arch Register"
  sources={{
    light: require('/img/arch-register/admin/members-light.png').default,
    dark: require('/img/arch-register/admin/members-dark.png').default,
  }}
/>

Open **Workspace Settings → Members** to review membership, filter by role or team, add an existing account, and assign
its workspace role. Creating a new local account additionally requires the global `admin_platform` permission and is
unavailable in OIDC mode. Removing a workspace member removes workspace access; it does not delete that person's
account or membership in other workspaces.

## Why They Are Separate

Keep these mental models distinct:

- a person can be a workspace member without owning content
- a team can own content without replacing workspace roles
- team-role assignment and workspace-role assignment solve different access problems
- application access policies can further limit access to optional applications
